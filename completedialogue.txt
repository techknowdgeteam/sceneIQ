# MAIN CHARACTER REFERENCE DEFINITION CATEGORY
## OFFICIAL PROTAGONIST CHARACTER MODEL, VISUAL IDENTITY AND REFERENCE GENERATION

### 1. PURPOSE AND AUTHORITY

This category establishes the permanent visual identity of the main protagonist in the POV explainer character system.

CHARACTER SPECIES ID: `white_round_headed_mascot`.

The protagonist is one individual of the project's ONLY fictional 2D cartoon mascot species. Every supporting character must belong to this same species and use the exact same underlying head, body, height, proportions, facial construction, white surfaces, and illustration style.

The protagonist's two exclusive identifiers are: (1) a completely bald, smooth white head, and (2) the blue downward-pointing forehead arrow. Supporting characters must use the same species and body model but must have visible hair and no arrow.

This is a stylized fictional mascot with a simple humanoid-shaped cartoon body, NOT a realistic human character or a separate human species. Never convert the protagonist into a photorealistic person, realistic 3D human, anime character, alternate cartoon species, animal-person, creature, robot, or different mascot.

The protagonist must use the exact cartoon model specified below. This is a fixed character model, not an optional design suggestion.

The protagonist must remain visually identical throughout:

- Stage 2 character extraction.
- Individual character reference generation.
- Stage 3 scene-by-scene generation.
- Every subsequent scene image.
- Every character appearance in the complete production.

This category is the authoritative definition of the main protagonist. Any older general character-design instruction that contradicts this category must be superseded by this definition.

### 2. OFFICIAL CHARACTER MODEL — BODY AND PROPORTIONS

The protagonist is a short, slightly stocky individual of the fictional `white_round_headed_mascot` species: a clean 2D illustrated mascot with a humanoid-shaped cartoon body, not realistic human anatomy.

**Fixed physical specifications:**

- Total height: exactly 4 feet (approximately 122 cm).
- Head: large, perfectly circular, smooth, solid white.
- Head-to-body proportion: approximately 40% head and 60% body.
- Body: properly developed, compact humanoid torso.
- Shoulders: moderately broad.
- Arms: proportionate, slightly rounded cartoon arms.
- Hands: white cartoon hands with clearly defined fingers.
- Waist: slightly rounded.
- Legs: short but proportionate.
- Thighs: moderately thick.
- Feet: simple cartoon feet or shoes.
- Outline: clean, bold black cartoon outlines.

**BODY BUILD — SLIGHTLY STOCKY**

The character must be slightly fat or stocky, but not extremely slim and not obese.

Give the character a gently rounded torso, moderately broad shoulders, moderately thick arms, a slightly rounded waist, and moderately thick thighs.

The character must not look like a thin stick figure, an extremely skinny cartoon, an obese character, or an exaggerated muscular character.

Maintain the same slightly stocky body proportions in every reference image and scene.

**HEAD-TO-BODY RATIO**

The head must remain large and perfectly round, but must not dominate the character.

The torso and legs must be sufficiently developed to create the same standardized mascot body silhouette used by every supporting character. Do not introduce realistic human anatomy or a different humanoid species.

Do not generate an exaggerated bobble-head model, an extremely tiny torso, or miniature legs. Do not generate a realistic human body, a second cartoon species, or an alternate mascot anatomy.

The same proportions must be used in front, side, rear, and three-quarter views.

### 3. HEAD DESIGN — COMPLETELY BALD

The protagonist must be completely bald.

The head must be:

- Perfectly circular.
- Smooth.
- Solid white.
- Cleanly outlined in black.
- Completely free from hair.

STRICTLY PROHIBITED:

- Hair of any colour.
- Hairlines.
- Fringes.
- Hairstyles.
- Sideburns.
- Beards.
- Moustaches.
- Facial hair.
- Hair-like decorative marks.

The bald head is a permanent and mandatory feature of the protagonist.

Do not assign hairstyles to the main character during character extraction, character reference generation, or Stage 3.

Even when the story describes the protagonist as having hair, the standardized cartoon model remains bald. Do not change this established visual identity.

### 4. MANDATORY FACIAL DESIGN — DOTTED EYES AND EYEBROWS

The protagonist must have a minimalist cartoon face.

**Eyes:**

- Exactly two small, solid-black circular dots.
- One dot on the left and one on the right.
- Both dots must be clearly visible.
- No horizontal lines passing through the dots.
- No horizontal lines beside the dots.
- No eye-crossing lines.
- No realistic pupils, eyelashes, or detailed eyelids.

**Eyebrows:**

- Two simple black cartoon eyebrows.
- Each eyebrow must sit directly above its corresponding dotted eye.
- Eyebrows must remain separate and clearly distinguishable from the eyes.
- Their angle and curvature may change to communicate emotions.

**Mouth:**

A small, simple black cartoon mouth.

Expressions may change according to the story, but the fundamental facial construction must remain identical.

**ABSOLUTE RULE:** The eyes must always remain simple black dots, with eyebrows above them. Never reintroduce horizontal eye-crossing lines.

### 5. BLUE AVATAR ARROW — OFFICIAL MAIN CHARACTER IDENTIFIER

The protagonist has one exclusive blue downward-pointing arrow attached to the surface of the round white head.

The arrow is a permanent identity marking.

It must never be interpreted as a floating icon, an arrow hovering above the head, or an arrow protruding beyond the head.

**ARROW APPEARANCE**

- Solid blue fill.
- Clean black outline.
- Rectangular shaft.
- Distinct downward-pointing triangular arrowhead.
- Arrowhead located on the upper forehead.
- Arrow positioned centrally on the head.
- The arrow must follow the actual visible surface of the head.

**CRITICAL SILHOUETTE RULE**

The arrow must remain inside the circular head silhouette wherever it is visible.

The outer circular outline of the head must remain the highest and widest boundary.

The arrow must never pump outward, extend beyond, protrude above, or distort the round silhouette of the head.

### 6. EXACT BLUE ARROW CONSTRUCTION IN THREE VIEWS

The same arrow must be interpreted correctly according to the camera angle.

**A. FRONT VIEW — COMPLETE FOREHEAD ARROW**

The front view must display the complete downward-pointing blue arrow.

The arrowhead must be positioned on the upper forehead, above the eyebrows.

The triangular arrowhead must point vertically downward toward the face.

The rectangular shaft must extend upward from the arrowhead toward the crown.

The top of the shaft must end inside the circular head boundary, leaving a small visible white margin between the shaft and the outer head outline.

There must be no blue section protruding above the top of the head.

The arrowhead must also remain completely inside the head silhouette.

The arrow must look like a marking on the forehead, not a floating avatar icon.

**B. SIDE VIEW — MINIMAL FOREHEAD VISIBILITY**

The side view must show the same character from a strict 90-degree side profile.

Because the arrowhead is positioned on the forehead, only a very small, naturally visible portion of the arrow may appear from the side.

Do not display a complete blue circular strip following the head.

Do not draw a blue line around the entire scalp.

Do not show the shaft running along the entire head outline.

Do not transform the arrow into a side-facing graphic.

The side view must retain the natural appearance of a circular bald white head, with only minimal arrow visibility at the front-facing forehead surface.

**C. REAR VIEW — SHORT UPPER-REAR SHAFT**

The rear view must show the plain white back of the head.

A small, narrow, vertical blue section of the rectangular arrow shaft may be visible at the upper centre of the rear head, near the crown.

This rear-visible portion must be SHORT.

It must:

- Remain within the upper portion of the rear head.
- Stop well before reaching the vertical centre of the back of the head.
- Never extend down to the neck.
- Never form a long vertical stripe.
- Never reach the lower half of the rear head.

There must be absolutely NO triangular arrowhead visible from behind.

The arrowhead belongs to the forehead, facing forward.

**D. ONE CONTINUOUS IDENTIFIER**

The front, side, and rear appearances must all represent the same arrow marking.

Do not generate different arrows for different views.

Do not invent a rear arrowhead.

Do not extend the arrow merely to make it more visible.

Correct perspective and correct anatomical placement take priority over making the arrow fully visible from every camera angle.

### 7. INDIVIDUAL CLOTHING AND CHARACTER DETAILS

The protagonist's clothing must be established from the actual story.

Clothing may change when the story establishes another occasion, profession, location, or time period.

However, the following must never change:

- Bald head.
- White circular head.
- White underlying body.
- Slightly stocky body build.
- 4-foot total height.
- Head-to-body proportions.
- Dotted eyes.
- Eyebrows.
- Blue forehead arrow and its exact view-dependent construction.

The reference-image prompt must establish one official default outfit based on the protagonist's story identity.

Clothing must fit the compact, slightly stocky humanoid body naturally.

### 8. OFFICIAL MAIN CHARACTER REFERENCE IMAGE GENERATION

For the protagonist, create an individual three-view character turnaround reference sheet.

It must contain:

1. FRONT VIEW — complete forehead arrow, face, full body, and outfit.
2. SIDE VIEW — strict side profile, minimal forehead-arrow visibility, and full body.
3. REAR VIEW — back of the head, short upper-rear arrow shaft, and full body.

All three views must show exactly the same individual.

Maintain the same height, body build, head dimensions, proportions, facial construction, clothing, and arrow design.

The character must be shown against a simple, clean, light background.

Use professional 2D cartoon illustration with bold black outlines, smooth white surfaces, natural cartoon clothing folds, and subtle dimensional shading.

Do not generate different characters or different versions of the protagonist.

### 9. STAGE 2 — CHARACTER EXTRACTION APPLICATION

During character extraction, this definition must be applied before the protagonist's individual appearance is designed.

The extraction system must:

- Identify the actual main protagonist from the complete story.
- Assign the protagonist the main-character reference classification.
- Apply this complete main-character model.
- Generate the character's story identity, role, relationships, and personality.
- Generate the protagonist's individual official clothing design.
- Preserve the bald head and slightly stocky body.
- Preserve the exact dotted-eye and eyebrow construction.
- Apply the exclusive blue arrow with the specified front, side, and rear appearance.
- Generate an independent three-view character reference image-generation prompt.
- Store the complete definition alongside the protagonist's individual appearance in the JSON data structure.

Do not independently invent another protagonist character model.

### 10. STAGE 3 — SCENE GENERATION APPLICATION

Master Prompt 3 must apply this complete definition whenever the protagonist appears.

For every scene containing the protagonist:

- Preserve the bald white round head.
- Preserve the slightly stocky body.
- Preserve the exact 4-foot character height and proportions.
- Preserve the dotted eyes without horizontal crossing lines.
- Preserve the eyebrows above the dotted eyes.
- Preserve the blue arrow according to the actual camera angle.
- Preserve the character's established clothing and individual identity.

Use the correct arrow appearance for front, side, rear, and three-quarter camera compositions.

Never remove the arrow simply because the camera angle changes.

Never force the arrow to appear fully visible from a side or rear perspective when its actual geometry would prevent that.

Never transfer the arrow to a supporting character.

This definition must also be repeated in every relevant character-reference block inside every scene JSON entry, not merely stored in the character library.

### 11. ABSOLUTE SPECIES EXCLUSIVITY AND IMAGE-GENERATION NEGATIVE RULES

Every time the protagonist is generated, the prompt must explicitly identify the species as `white_round_headed_mascot`.

The protagonist must visually match the same master species model used by every supporting character. The protagonist's bald head and arrow are identity-specific traits, not permission to alter the head shape, body, facial style, or species.

STRICTLY PROHIBITED IN EVERY PROTAGONIST IMAGE:

- Photorealistic humans, realistic human faces, realistic human anatomy, realistic skin, realistic human proportions, or photographic people.
- Anime, realistic 3D humans, semi-realistic human cartoon styles, or unrelated cartoon species.
- Animal-person, anthropomorphic animal, fantasy creature, monster, robot, alien, or a second mascot design.
- A different head shape, head size, body height, 40:60 ratio, body width, limb proportions, hands, feet, outline style, or facial construction.
- Hair, a hairline, fringe, beard, moustache, sideburns, or any other hair on the protagonist.
- Removing, redesigning, recolouring, floating, relocating, or transferring the exclusive blue arrow.

The protagonist is the only bald character and the only arrow-bearing character in the entire cast. Supporting characters are the same species but must have clearly visible hair and no arrow.

The presence of story roles such as husband, wife, doctor, police officer, executive, child, or elderly parent must never cause the image generator to switch to realistic human people. Render every person as the standardized mascot species.

### 12. FINAL MAIN-CHARACTER SPECIES VALIDATION

Before approving any protagonist reference or scene image, verify:

1. `character_species_id` is exactly `white_round_headed_mascot`.
2. The character uses the shared 4-foot, slightly stocky, 40:60 mascot body model.
3. The head is perfectly round, smooth, solid white, and bold-black-outlined.
4. The protagonist is completely bald with no hair or facial hair.
5. The two eyes are black dots, with separate eyebrows above them and a simple mouth.
6. The exclusive blue arrow is correctly attached to the forehead and follows the required view-dependent construction.
7. No realistic human, alternative species, creature, animal-person, robot, alien, anime, or realistic 3D design appears.
8. The image preserves the exact same species model used by supporting characters.

If any check fails, correct the image-generation prompt before continuing.

# OTHER CHARACTERS REFERENCE DEFINITION CATEGORY
## OFFICIAL SUPPORTING CHARACTER MODEL, VISUAL IDENTITY AND REFERENCE GENERATION

### 1. PURPOSE AND AUTHORITY

This category establishes the permanent visual identity of every meaningful supporting character in the story.

**CHARACTER SPECIES ID: `white_round_headed_mascot`.**

All supporting characters must belong to exactly the same fictional 2D cartoon mascot species as the main protagonist. They are not realistic humans, a second human species, alternative cartoon species, or separate humanoid designs. The shared model is fixed; only approved identity details may differ.

The protagonist and supporting characters are individuals of ONE species. The protagonist alone is bald and has the exclusive blue arrow. Every supporting character must have visible hair and no arrow.

This category is authoritative for Stage 2 character extraction and Stage 3 scene generation. It overrides generic instructions, character stereotypes, and any story description that might otherwise suggest a realistic human or alternate character design.

### 2. ABSOLUTE SINGLE-SPECIES RULE — NO REALISTIC HUMAN OR ALTERNATE CHARACTER SPECIES

There is only ONE permitted species for all person-characters in this production:

`white_round_headed_mascot`

Every supporting character—regardless of gender, age, profession, personality, relationship, social status, nationality, or narrative importance—must be an individual of the exact same mascot species as the protagonist.

The species is a clean, stylized 2D cartoon mascot with a humanoid-shaped cartoon body. The word humanoid describes the mascot's simplified cartoon body layout only. It does NOT permit an ordinary realistic human character or a separate human species.

STRICTLY PROHIBITED:

- Photorealistic humans or realistic human characters.
- Realistic human faces, skin tones, facial anatomy, body anatomy, or photographic people.
- Realistic 3D humans, semi-realistic human characters, anime humans, or human characters from another illustration style.
- Different cartoon species, animal-people, anthropomorphic animals, fantasy humanoids, monsters, aliens, robots, creatures, or unrelated mascots used to portray people.
- Different head shapes, head dimensions, body builds, heights, body proportions, limb proportions, hands, feet, or outline weights.
- Realistic noses, ears, realistic teeth, realistic pupils, eyelashes, detailed eyelids, skin tones, or facial features that conflict with the locked minimalist mascot face.

A person's occupation, age, gender, personality, wealth, status, or story function must be communicated through hairstyle, hair colour, clothing, uniform, accessories, professional equipment, expression, posture, and story context only. These details must never change the species or fundamental anatomy.

If the story says “a human man,” “a woman,” “a doctor,” “a child,” “an elderly person,” “a police officer,” or any other person-role, depict that person as this same mascot species. Never render them as realistic humans.

A real animal may be shown as an animal only when the story genuinely contains an animal as an animal. Never use an animal or creature design to portray a person or replace a human role.

### 3. OFFICIAL SHARED CHARACTER MODEL — BODY AND PROPORTIONS

Every supporting character must use exactly the same underlying model as the protagonist.

**Fixed physical specifications:**

- Total height: exactly 4 feet (approximately 122 cm).
- Head: large, perfectly circular, smooth, solid white, with a bold black outline.
- Head-to-body proportion: approximately 40% head and 60% body.
- Body: compact, slightly stocky white cartoon torso.
- Shoulders: moderately broad, exactly matching the protagonist's model.
- Arms: proportionate, slightly rounded cartoon arms.
- Hands: small white cartoon hands with clearly defined simple fingers.
- Waist: slightly rounded.
- Legs: short but proportionate.
- Thighs: moderately thick.
- Feet: simple cartoon feet or shoes.
- Outline: clean, bold black cartoon outlines consistent with the protagonist.

**BODY BUILD — IDENTICAL TO THE PROTAGONIST**

Every supporting character must have exactly the same body silhouette and proportions as the protagonist. Do not make supporting characters thinner, fatter, taller, shorter, wider, more muscular, more anatomically realistic, or differently proportioned.

Do not use age, gender, profession, or personality to change body shape. Older and younger supporting characters still use the same locked mascot body model.

The only allowed differences are visible hairstyle, clothing, clothing colours, accessories, professional equipment, facial expression, posture, and story-supported identity details.

### 4. HEAD DESIGN — SAME ROUND WHITE HEAD, WITH VISIBLE HAIR REQUIRED

Every supporting character must have the same perfectly round, smooth, solid-white head, same dimensions, and same bold black outline as the protagonist.

Unlike the protagonist, every supporting character MUST have clearly visible hair.

**MANDATORY SUPPORTING-CHARACTER HAIR RULE:**

- Every supporting character must be assigned a distinct, visible hairstyle in the official reference design.
- `hair_present` must be `true`.
- `is_bald` must be `false`.
- The scalp must not appear bare or completely hairless.
- Never generate a bald, shaved, clean-shaven-scalp, skin-fade-to-bare-scalp, or hairless supporting character.
- Never use a hairstyle so short or faint that the person reads as bald.
- If the story describes a supporting character as bald, shaved, or hairless, the standardized supporting-character design rule still takes priority: give that character a visible, story-appropriate hairstyle while preserving their role and clothing identity.

Suitable hairstyles include:

- Short neatly arranged hair.
- Short textured hair with visible texture.
- Side-parted hair.
- Curly or wavy hair.
- Straight hair.
- Medium-length hair.
- Long hair.
- Braids.
- Ponytails.
- Buns.
- Distinct professional hairstyles.
- Other clearly visible hair arrangements appropriate to the character.

Hair must follow the round head's natural contour. It must not change the head dimensions, shape, species, or body proportions. Hair must not cover the mandatory facial features unnecessarily.

A hat, helmet, hood, or head covering may partially obscure hair in a particular story scene. The underlying official supporting-character design must still define clearly visible hair, and the image-generation prompt must preserve the same hair identity wherever visible.

### 5. MANDATORY FACIAL DESIGN — SAME DOTTED EYES AND EYEBROWS

Every supporting character must have exactly the same facial construction as the protagonist.

**Eyes:**

- Exactly two small, solid-black circular dots.
- One eye on the left and one on the right.
- No horizontal lines through the dotted eyes.
- No horizontal lines beside the eyes.
- No eye-crossing lines.
- No realistic pupils, eyelashes, or detailed eyelids.

**Eyebrows:**

- Two simple black cartoon eyebrows.
- One positioned above each dotted eye.
- Their angles and curvature may vary to communicate emotions.

**Mouth:**

A small, simple black cartoon mouth using the same design language as the protagonist.

The facial expression may change, but the fundamental face must remain part of the same mascot species.

Never introduce realistic human facial anatomy, realistic nose or ear detail, human skin tones, detailed human eyes, or another facial style.

### 6. ABSOLUTE NO-BLUE-ARROW RULE

Supporting characters must have NO BLUE AVATAR ARROW or substitute identifying mark.

The protagonist's blue arrow is an exclusive identity feature and must never be copied to a supporting character.

STRICTLY PROHIBITED:

- Blue triangular arrowheads.
- Blue rectangular arrow shafts.
- Blue forehead markings.
- Blue crown markings.
- Blue rear-head markings.
- Floating avatar arrows.
- Any symbol resembling the protagonist's arrow.

This prohibition applies in front, side, rear, and three-quarter views; full-body references; close-ups; group compositions; and all story scenes.

A supporting character's hair must not resemble or imitate the blue arrow. Do not use a blue arrow-shaped hairstyle, blue pointed crown, or arrow-like head decoration.

### 7. INDIVIDUAL CLOTHING AND CHARACTER DIFFERENTIATION

Every supporting character must have a specific, story-appropriate official appearance.

Differentiate individuals using:

- Clearly visible hairstyle and hair arrangement.
- Hair length, texture, and colour where appropriate.
- Clothing type, design, and colours.
- Profession-specific attire or uniforms.
- Footwear.
- Relevant accessories.
- Professional equipment.
- Small story-supported details.

Examples:

- Doctor: medical coat or appropriate medical uniform.
- Police officer: recognizable cartoon police uniform.
- Business executive: professional suit or business attire.
- Engineer: practical engineering or professional work clothing.
- Teacher: smart-casual or professional teaching attire.
- Student: appropriate student clothing.
- Construction worker: practical workwear, safety vest, hard hat, and suitable protective equipment.
- Lawyer: professional business clothing.
- Mechanic: practical workwear.
- Parent: story-appropriate everyday clothing.

All these occupations must still be represented by the SAME white round-headed mascot species and exactly the same base anatomy.

For every character, define the main outfit, upper-body clothing, lower-body clothing, clothing colours, footwear, accessories, and any profession-related equipment. Be specific rather than writing only “casual clothes” or “professional attire.”

Each character must have an official default outfit. Stage 3 may change clothing only when the story establishes a different occasion, time period, or activity. Preserve the same underlying species and individual identity.

### 8. FEMALE, MALE, YOUNGER AND OLDER SUPPORTING CHARACTERS

Every gender and age presentation must use the same mascot species and same base model.

Do not create a realistic human woman, realistic human man, realistic child, or realistic elderly person. Do not change head shape, head-to-body ratio, height, stockiness, limb structure, facial construction, or illustration style.

Supporting characters must all have visible hair. Use hairstyle, hair arrangement, clothing, accessories, posture, and simple facial expression to distinguish gender presentation and story role.

Do not exaggerate human anatomical features or create a different body model for female or male characters.

### 9. PROFESSIONS AND CHARACTER ROLES

Extract each supporting character's role and profession from the story. Do not assume a profession without evidence. If unspecified, record “Not specified in the story.”

Roles may include spouse, parent, child, sibling, friend, partner, employer, employee, manager, doctor, nurse, police officer, detective, lawyer, teacher, student, engineer, construction worker, security guard, customer, neighbour, investor, business owner, or other meaningful people.

Role-based clothing may communicate occupation, but must not create a new character species or realistic-human design.

### 10. THREE-VIEW SUPPORTING CHARACTER REFERENCE GENERATION

Every supporting character must receive an individual three-view reference sheet showing the same individual:

1. FRONT VIEW — full face, clearly visible hairstyle, full body, clothing, hands, and feet.
2. SIDE VIEW — strict 90-degree side profile showing the same round head, visible hairstyle, and unchanged body proportions.
3. REAR VIEW — rear head shape and hairstyle, clothing, arms, hands, legs, and feet.

All three views must preserve exactly the same:

- Character species ID: `white_round_headed_mascot`.
- 4-foot height.
- Head shape and dimensions.
- 40:60 head-to-body proportion.
- Slightly stocky body build.
- Dotted eyes and separate eyebrow construction wherever the face is visible.
- Individual visible hairstyle.

The sheet must clearly show that the supporting character has hair. No view may depict the supporting character as bald or shaved. No blue arrow may appear in any view.

Use a simple, unobtrusive background and a neutral standing pose. Do not create realistic human anatomy or an alternate species.

Do not allow a character's real-world description to override the mandatory mascot model. If the story describes a bald supporting person, the generated supporting-character design must nevertheless contain visible hair.
